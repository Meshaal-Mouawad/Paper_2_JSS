# LEAP Paper 2 — Autonomous Scientific Recovery, Published-Research Benchmarking, and SCP Submission Preparation

## 1. Mission

Act as a Senior Scientific Research Director, an experienced Editor-in-Chief, and a specialist in empirical software engineering.

Your assignment is to independently examine, strengthen, and prepare LEAP Paper 2 for **Science of Computer Programming (SCP)**, following its editorial rejection from the Journal of Systems and Software (JSS).

This is a scientific improvement assignment, not simply a manuscript rewriting task.

The objective is to establish a defensible contribution to published software engineering knowledge, supported by the completed experiments and verified research evidence.

**JSS manuscript:** JSSOFTWARE-D-26-02929

**Submitted title:** When Generated KPI Explanations Misrepresent Source Code: An Empirical Evaluation of Automated Checks

**JSS decision:** Rejected during editorial pre-screening. The editor stated that the paper was not yet ready for a leading journal and recommended obtaining additional research feedback. No detailed technical criticisms were supplied.

Treat that decision as serious evidence that the manuscript requires scientific reassessment. Do not attribute unreported criticisms to the editor.

## 9. Review assignment E — Reconstruct and revise the manuscript

After establishing the scientific contribution, revise the manuscript as one connected explanation.

Follow this progression:

**Research problem → existing research → unresolved question → our proposed response → methodology → evidence → findings → scientific implications.**

The reader is technically educated but has never encountered LEAP.

Never introduce an unfamiliar concept, symbol, acronym, component, or comparison without first explaining its meaning and relevance.

Use direct subjects and familiar technical language.

Introduce researchers by describing what they investigated and established before discussing their frameworks.

In the literature review, connect research contributions logically. Explain what each significant study adds and how it relates to the present problem.

Make every paragraph develop one clear idea and prepare the next.

Replace unclear wording rather than adding explanatory paragraphs that repeat the same information.

Use consistent terminology throughout the text, figures, tables, and captions.

Avoid unexplained symbols, expressions such as "Exact / n", vague claims, abrupt mathematical notation, and technical descriptions that assume the reader already knows the study.

A table must explain itself through its title, headings, and caption.

Do not place editorial responses, reviewer negotiations, or revision-history explanations inside the clean manuscript.

Concentrate the manuscript on its strongest scientific contribution.
