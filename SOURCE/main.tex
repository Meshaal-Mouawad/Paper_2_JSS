\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Context:} Source linkage and explanation fidelity are distinct verification properties of software-derived documentation. A publication can preserve the correct source relationship while misrepresenting the source role, executable conditions, representation disagreement, or review authority.
\textbf{Objective:} We operationalize this distinction as four auditable publication obligations---source-role accuracy, condition disclosure, representation-disagreement preservation, and review-authority consistency---and evaluate their ability to localize publication defects and transfer across documentation generators.
\textbf{Method:} Deterministic rules audit a frozen 28-dossier LEAP publication, an earlier 18-dossier state, and two additional LEAP input configurations. The checker is exercised against 18 predeclared rule fixtures and 14 artifact-derived controls. An external pilot reads expected roles and constraints directly from four official FastAPI source examples, compares them with generated OpenAPI 3.1 descriptions, and confirms runtime enforcement for constrained inputs.
\textbf{Results:} All 32 checker-validation outcomes match their expected labels. The current LEAP audit localizes three generator defect classes: lineage typing in 4 dossiers with 12 visible manifestations, guard-to-safeguard binding in 5 of 10 eligible guards, and unconditional certification language in all 19 unresolved dossiers. All 6 eligible explicit-conflict dossiers retain Needs Review and Review Queue routing. The earlier LEAP state preserves the same 4 lineage-affected dossiers and the same 6 conflict-routed dossiers. In FastAPI, all 7 directly applicable source-role and condition checks pass, 9 cells receive N/A through the applicability rules, and 2 lifecycle-status analogue checks pass.
\textbf{Conclusion:} The evidence demonstrates that provenance-preserving publication still requires semantic verification at the reader-facing boundary. The resulting audit procedure separates source identity from explanation fidelity, localizes generator repair targets, and transfers the source-role and declarative-condition checks to an independently developed OpenAPI generator.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Source linkage and explanation fidelity are distinct verification properties of generated software documentation. The frozen LEAP publication provides direct evidence: its dossiers retain source evidence while generated text can misclassify a function docstring as an output alias, reduce a non-positive guard to generic zero-handling prose, or place certification-oriented language beside unresolved review state. These cases establish the engineering problem addressed in this paper: publication quality depends on both provenance and faithful communication of what the linked evidence means.

Prior research supplies the foundations for evaluating that communication. Code--text consistency and documentation equivalence address behavioral fidelity \citep{Wen2019,Xu2024,Eghbali2026}; API directive analysis addresses parameter constraints and exceptions \citep{Zhou2017}; inconsistency management preserves competing representations \citep{Nuseibeh2001}; evidence models distinguish evidence and assertion roles \citep{Brush2016,Giglio2019}; and provenance and governance frameworks represent lineage, validity, and publication state \citep{Missier2013,W3CProvConstraints2013,Psallidas2023,Sadiq2026}. We integrate these concerns at the publication boundary where source evidence, declared representations, generated explanation, and status metadata appear together for a reader.

We call the resulting property \emph{explanation fidelity} and operationalize it through four obligations: source-role accuracy, condition disclosure, representation-disagreement preservation, and review-authority consistency. The selection follows three explicit criteria: each obligation is grounded in prior software-engineering or provenance research; each corresponds to a distinct publication transformation observable in the frozen artifact; and each admits a deterministic failure condition. This yields an executable audit contract rather than an informal review checklist.

The evaluation combines three evidence layers. First, a complete-corpus LEAP audit classifies publication manifestations and traces repeated manifestations to generator mechanisms. Second, checker controls and frozen LEAP states test deterministic behavior, temporal persistence, and input sensitivity. Third, an external FastAPI/OpenAPI pilot reads expected roles and constraints from independently authored source, then compares those values with independently generated publication artifacts and runtime responses. Together these layers test the paper's central claim and the portability of the audit procedure.

The paper contributes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item an executable four-obligation contract for explanation fidelity at a multi-representation software publication boundary;
\item complete-corpus localization of three LEAP generator defect classes and measured preservation of explicit-conflict routing;
\item a provenance-presence baseline showing that correct source linkage can coexist with explanation-fidelity failures;
\item checker-validation, temporal-persistence, and input-sensitivity evidence tied to frozen artifacts; and
\item an external FastAPI/OpenAPI application in which the source-role and declarative-condition checks transfer directly through explicit adapters.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Claims and falsification conditions}
The study evaluates four major claims under explicit failure conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{C1---Source linkage and explanation fidelity are distinct verification properties.} Evidence: the source-linked Yield and Gross Margin dossiers retain provenance while exhibiting applicable source-role, condition, representation, or authority failures. Falsification condition: every source-linked audited case preserves all applicable explanation-fidelity obligations.
\item \textbf{C2---The current LEAP corpus contains three generator defect classes.} Evidence: 4 dossiers with 12 lineage manifestations, 5 guard-binding failures among 10 eligible guards, 19 authority-language failures among 19 unresolved dossiers, and root-cause excerpts mapping each class to one generator mechanism. Falsification condition: a frozen-corpus rerun changes the class assignments or the root-cause mapping fails to reproduce the reported manifestations.
\item \textbf{C3---Explicit-conflict routing preserves disagreement in the eligible LEAP cases.} Evidence: all 6 current eligible conflict dossiers retain \emph{Needs Review} and Review Queue, and the same 6 named cases persist in the earlier state. Falsification condition: any eligible explicit-conflict dossier loses either routing element.
\item \textbf{C4---Source-role and declarative-condition checks transfer to the FastAPI/OpenAPI publication path.} Evidence: all 7 directly applicable checks pass; official source declarations, OpenAPI fields, negative role mutations, and HTTP 422 responses provide observable counterparts. Falsification condition: any directly applicable official example fails the source-to-publication comparison under the released adapter.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Derivation of the Publication Contract}
\subsection{Source roles and behavioral fidelity}
Software documentation quality depends on semantic agreement between code and text \citep{Aghajani2019,Wen2019,Xu2024}. Eghbali et al. formalize code-documentation equivalence around accurate and complete descriptions of program behavior \citep{Eghbali2026}. Zhou et al. compare API code with parameter-constraint and exception directives to detect documentation defects \citep{Zhou2017}. These lines of work establish source-role accuracy and condition disclosure as independently inspectable publication responsibilities.

\subsection{Representation disagreement and authority}
Software artifacts can retain inconsistent representations while managing their consequences explicitly \citep{Nuseibeh2001}. SEPIO represents claims, evidence lines, methods, tools, and supporting or refuting relations \citep{Brush2016}; ECO distinguishes evidence and assertion-method types \citep{Giglio2019}. These models support two additional publication responsibilities: explicit disagreement must remain visible, and reader-facing authority language must correspond to the recorded review state.

\subsection{Provenance and source-bound publication}
PROV supplies entity--activity--agent provenance and formal validity constraints \citep{Missier2013,W3CProvConstraints2013}. OneProvenance reconstructs dynamic query lineage from database events \citep{Psallidas2023}, and ESDPKI combines semantic provenance with validation-gated publication and governance \citep{Sadiq2026}. Source-bound software-knowledge proposals extend the same direction toward evidence-linked projections and explicit validation state \citep{Gao2026,MacedoCosta2026}. The present contract connects these provenance structures to reader-facing explanation checks.

KPI semantics provide the domain in which the contract is evaluated. Semantic KPI research formalizes indicator definitions and derivations \citep{Mate2017,RoldanGarcia2021,Diamantini2025}, while AFFECTK makes traceability and consistency explicit during KPI evolution \citep{Dominguez2025}. LEAP adds a publication surface where these representations, source evidence, and review state can be inspected together.

\section{Research Questions and Method}
The study asks:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2cm,style=nextline]
\item[RQ1] Which publication defect classes and preserved mechanisms are exposed by the four-obligation audit in the current complete LEAP dossier build, and which generator mechanisms explain them?
\item[RQ2] Which manifestations persist across an earlier LEAP state and alternative LEAP input configurations, and how does input variation change the observed classes?
\item[RQ3] Which parts of the publication-audit procedure instantiate directly on an independently developed FastAPI/OpenAPI generator, and what adapter behavior is required?
\end{description}

\subsection{LEAP corpora}
The primary corpus contains all 28 rendered dossier HTML pages from the frozen LEAP sample-project publication artifact: 9 carry \emph{Validated} status and 19 carry \emph{Needs Review}. A public artifact copy at commit \texttt{5e697b5b} matches the 28 submitted pages byte-for-byte by SHA-256. The earlier archived state contains 18 rendered dossiers: 6 \emph{Validated} and 12 \emph{Needs Review}. Two additional frozen input configurations use the same generator: a 38-file scenario-fixture scope producing 29 dossier publication sources and an 8-file challenge scope producing 8.

The unit of analysis is a generated dossier. For temporal analysis, named manifestations are compared across the current and earlier frozen states. For input-sensitivity analysis, the scenario and challenge configurations are audited with the same released rules.

\subsection{External FastAPI/OpenAPI corpus}
The external corpus uses four official FastAPI tutorial source files from the public \texttt{fastapi/fastapi} repository at commit \texttt{33d411dbc3236275dd64d200bfe18d5d60a49b2e}. The examples cover a constrained query parameter, constrained path/query parameters, constrained request-body fields, and a deprecated path operation. Their Git blob identities and SHA-256 values are retained in the supplement. The released environment pins FastAPI 0.128.2 and Pydantic 2.13.4 and archives the generated OpenAPI 3.1 descriptions and runtime validation outcomes \citep{FastAPI2026,OpenAPI31}.

Expected external roles and constraints are read directly from the official source declarations. Source-role accuracy maps to OpenAPI parameter location, parameter name, or request-body placement. Condition disclosure maps to generated minimum, maximum, pattern, and field constraints. The applicability layer assigns N/A when an external example lacks the state required by a given obligation. Lifecycle/deprecation propagation is reported as a separate status analogue.

\subsection{Deterministic audit rules}
The released audit library implements four predicates.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Source-role accuracy.} A failure is recorded when a published role/name disagrees with the source role/name. LEAP-specific parsing also detects comments or docstrings assigned executable roles and SQL \texttt{CAST} types assigned as output aliases.
\item \textbf{Condition disclosure.} Eligible LEAP cases contain explicit zero or non-positive denominator guards expressed as \texttt{NULLIF(variable,0)} or an \texttt{if}-family comparison to zero. Preservation requires a nonempty displayed denominator, variable correspondence, affirmative handling semantics, and non-positive wording for \texttt{<=0} guards. FastAPI uses the same comparison principle over declarative validation constraints.
\item \textbf{Representation-disagreement preservation.} Eligible LEAP pages carry an explicit conflict, mismatch, or inversion marker. Preservation requires \emph{Needs Review} and Review Queue routing.
\item \textbf{Review-authority consistency.} Eligible pages carry \emph{Needs Review}. A failure occurs when the same page contains bounded certification-oriented phrases such as "Certified metric dossier", "certified calculation expression", or "Print Certified Dossier".
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Checker validation and audit execution}
The condition rule evolved through retained protocols. R3 replaced the earlier 11-row manual condition table with deterministic rules before the later corpus reruns. R4 negative-control testing exposed two checker faults: unconditional acceptance of one external query-role cell and guard acceptance with missing denominator or contradictory safeguard wording. The released checker repairs both behaviors.

Validation uses two oracle-separated layers. Eighteen predeclared rule fixtures cover correct and substituted source roles, denominator binding, contradictory safeguard prose, loss of non-positive semantics, conflict routing, authority language, benign controls, and N/A behavior. Fourteen artifact-derived controls add five unchanged baselines, eight extracted-input substitutions, and one document-level HTML mutation reparsed through the production publication adapter. The release requires all oracle comparisons and all corpus summaries to pass.

The primary audit then verifies corpus identity, status counts, lineage pairs, guard instances, conflict routing, certification wording, and root-cause source excerpts. Repeated page manifestations are grouped into a defect class only when a common frozen generator mechanism explains them.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Current LEAP corpus: three defect classes and preserved conflict routing}
The 28-dossier audit localizes three generator defect classes. Lineage typing affects 4 dossiers with 12 visible manifestations. Guard-to-safeguard binding fails in 5 of 10 eligible guards and preserves 5. Authority-language consistency fails in all 19 unresolved dossiers. Explicit-conflict routing preserves all 6 eligible cases through both \emph{Needs Review} and Review Queue.

The alternative configurations reproduce lineage and authority failures under changed inputs. The 29-dossier scenario build contains one lineage-affected dossier, authority-language failures on all 11 unresolved dossiers, one preserved eligible guard, and 2/2 preserved explicit conflicts. The 8-dossier challenge build contains one lineage-affected dossier, authority-language failures on all 6 unresolved dossiers, one preserved eligible guard, and 5/5 preserved explicit conflicts. These results identify guard binding as input-sensitive within the evaluated configurations, while lineage, authority language, and conflict routing recur under the alternative inputs.

\subsection{Root-cause localization}
The lineage manifestations map to the developer-lineage classifier: calculation/formula keywords are tested before comment typing, the ABAP \texttt{*} comment prefix is omitted from comment recognition, and a generic SQL \texttt{AS} pattern can select a \texttt{CAST} type instead of the final alias. One classifier mechanism therefore explains the repeated source-role failures.

The guard manifestations map to safeguard generation. The generator recognizes \texttt{NULLIF} and selected equality-to-zero patterns and emits generic zero-denominator prose. Variable-to-denominator binding and \texttt{<=0} semantics are absent from that generation step, producing the observed failures while aligned \texttt{NULLIF} cases pass.

The authority manifestations map to certification strings emitted independently of review state in generator prose and the page template. Conflict routing follows a separate mechanism that assigns explicit conflicts to \emph{Needs Review} and the Review Queue; this accounts for the 6/6 preserved conflict cases.

\subsection{Checker validation}
All 18 predeclared rule fixtures and all 14 artifact-derived controls match their expected labels. Across the 30 binary Pass/Fail controls, the checker records zero misses on deliberately introduced defects and zero false alarms on benign controls. Two additional fixtures verify N/A behavior. The repaired cases include wrong external parameter location/name, missing displayed denominator, contradictory safeguard prose, heading-only safeguard text reparsed through the production adapter, suppressed Review Queue routing, and certification language beside unresolved status.

\subsection{External FastAPI/OpenAPI application}
All 7 directly applicable source-role and condition checks pass across the four official examples. Nine of the 16 example-by-obligation cells receive N/A through the applicability rules. Two negative mutations of the query role/name are rejected. Two separately labeled lifecycle-status analogue checks pass: source-level \texttt{deprecated=True} is propagated to OpenAPI \texttt{deprecated=true}. Invalid inputs for the three constrained examples return HTTP 422, matching the constraints represented in the generated descriptions.

\section{Detailed Case Analysis and Provenance Baseline}
\subsection{Ethylene Production Yield}
The Yield source contains a percentage calculation and a non-positive-input guard:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python,float=htbp,caption={Selected Yield source retained in the dossier.}]
def calculate_ethylene_yield_pct(ethylene_produced_tons,
                                 feedstock_input_tons):
    """Calculates ethylene production yield as a percentage."""
    if feedstock_input_tons <= 0:
        return 0.0
    return (ethylene_produced_tons / feedstock_input_tons) * 100.0
\end{lstlisting}
The dossier retains the source path and source text and therefore passes the provenance-presence baseline. Its generated lineage prose labels the function docstring as an output alias, and its safeguard prose loses the non-positive-input semantics. The same source-linked page therefore fails source-role accuracy and condition disclosure.

\subsection{Gross Margin Percentage}
The Gross Margin dossier retains the declared formula
\begin{equation}
D=100\frac{R-C}{R},
\end{equation}
while the implementation, after guarding zero cost, returns
\begin{equation}
I=100\frac{R}{C}.
\end{equation}
For $R>0$ and $C>0$,
\begin{align}
I-D &=100\left(\frac{R}{C}+\frac{C}{R}-1\right)\\
&=100\left(1+\frac{(R-C)^2}{RC}\right)\ge 100.
\end{align}
The implemented expression therefore exceeds the declaration by at least 100 percentage points throughout the strictly positive domain, with equality in the lower bound at $R=C$. At positive revenue and zero cost, the declaration evaluates to 100 while the implementation guard returns zero; at zero revenue and positive cost, the declaration is undefined while the implementation returns zero.

The dossier retains both source and declared representation and therefore passes the provenance-presence baseline. It also preserves the explicit disagreement through \emph{Needs Review} and Review Queue. The same page fails guard binding and review-authority consistency because its safeguard prose describes a generic zero denominator and certification-oriented language accompanies the unresolved status.

\subsection{What the provenance baseline adds}
The two detailed cases produce the same baseline pattern: source identity and local provenance are present, yet the explanation-fidelity audit detects publication-semantic failures. This paired evidence directly supports C1. Provenance answers which artifact a statement came from; the four-obligation audit verifies whether the publication describes that artifact and its state faithfully.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Answers to the research questions}
\textbf{RQ1.} The complete current LEAP corpus exposes three generator defect classes and one preserved routing mechanism. Root-cause tracing maps lineage failures to classification order and syntax recognition, guard failures to missing variable/condition binding, and authority failures to unconditional certification templates. All 6 eligible explicit conflicts retain the intended review routing.

\textbf{RQ2.} The earlier LEAP state preserves the same 4 lineage-affected dossiers with 12 manifestations and the same 6 conflict-routed dossiers. Guard outcomes change from 5/10 failures to 4/9, while authority language affects every unresolved dossier in both states. The scenario and challenge configurations reproduce lineage and authority failures and preserve their eligible guards, showing that the evaluated guard defect depends on the specific input patterns reaching the generator.

\textbf{RQ3.} The FastAPI/OpenAPI application instantiates the source-role and declarative-condition checks directly. Expected values come from official FastAPI source declarations; generated OpenAPI fields and runtime responses provide the comparison surface. Seven applicable checks pass, wrong role/name mutations fail, and constrained invalid requests return HTTP 422. The applicability mechanism assigns N/A to obligations whose required state is absent from a selected example.

\subsection{Engineering implications}
The root causes yield three concrete repair targets. Source-line explanation should type a statement before generating role-specific prose. Safeguard generation should bind each condition to the variable and mathematical representation it governs. Certification language should be conditional on review state. The conflict-routing path should remain intact because it preserves explicit disagreement for review.

The audit also separates page manifestation from generator cause. A page-level finding identifies where communication fails; root-cause grouping identifies the implementation mechanism responsible for repeated manifestations. This distinction converts a long list of page defects into a small set of repairable generator classes.

The external application shows how adapters preserve the same verification logic across publication models. OpenAPI offers explicit parameter locations, names, schemas, and deprecation metadata \citep{OpenAPI31}; these fields provide direct counterparts for source-role and declarative-condition checks. Applicability gates allow the audit to represent absent state as N/A and keep each obligation tied to observable evidence.

\section{Conclusion}
Source linkage and explanation fidelity are separate verification properties at the software-publication boundary. The complete LEAP audit demonstrates this separation by locating source-linked pages whose generated explanation misstates source role, guard semantics, or review authority. The audit reduces those manifestations to three generator defect classes and confirms preservation of all 6 eligible explicit conflicts through review routing.

The checker-validation layer reproduces all 32 expected outcomes, and the earlier and alternative LEAP states expose which manifestations persist under time and input variation. The external FastAPI/OpenAPI application then carries the source-role and declarative-condition checks to an independently developed generator, with 7/7 applicable checks passing, negative role mutations rejected, and runtime constraint behavior matching the generated descriptions.

The resulting contribution is an executable publication-audit method that complements provenance with semantic checks over source role, conditions, disagreement, and authority. Its output identifies concrete generator repair targets and provides falsifiable evidence for whether reader-facing documentation preserves the meaning and state of its linked software evidence.

\section{Limitations}
The four obligations define an operational subset of explanation fidelity centered on source role, condition disclosure, explicit representation disagreement, and review authority. Dimensions such as freshness, accessibility, multilingual paraphrase, and broader semantic completeness remain future extensions of the contract.

The checker controls are author-developed edge cases tied to the released predicates. Independent human annotation and held-out cross-project defect corpora remain future validation steps. A tool-to-tool baseline against documentation linters, OpenAPI validators, and semantic inconsistency detectors also remains a future comparative evaluation.

The historical and alternative LEAP corpora are states and configurations of the same generator. Their role in this study is temporal persistence and input sensitivity. The external FastAPI corpus contains four official examples and directly exercises source-role and declarative-condition behavior; the selected examples contain no explicit competing representation and no human review state, so those two obligations receive N/A. Generator repair-and-rerun evaluation remains a separate engineering follow-up to the localized root causes.
